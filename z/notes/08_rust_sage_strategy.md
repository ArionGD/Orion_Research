Here is the difficulty scorecard for building your own offline Rust Pair-Programmer.

### 1. Base Model Selection
**Difficulty: 2 / 10**
You do not need to build the brain. You use **DeepSeek Coder V2** or **StarCoder2**. These are open-source models already pre-trained on billions of lines of code. They are "The Raw Silicon."

### 2. The Rust Knowledge Base (Data Curation)
**Difficulty: 8 / 10 (The Core Challenge)**
Rust is unique because of the **Borrow Checker** and **Ownership rules**. Most AI models generate "hallucinated" Rust code that refuses to compile. 
*   **The Hard Part:** To make a "Sage," you must scrape the latest **Rust Book**, every major crate's documentation (Tokio, Serde, Actix), and thousands of successful GitHub Pull Requests that fix "Borrow Checker" errors. 
*   **Your Moat:** If you can feed your AI "Before vs. After" code—showing a common Rust error and the exact expert fix—your AI will become smarter than a human Junior Dev.

### 3. Fine-Tuning (Training Architecture)
**Difficulty: 6 / 10**
Using a technique called **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**, you can fine-tune a massive model using a single, high-end consumer GPU (like an Nvidia RTX 4090). You don't need a supercomputer. 

### 4. Local Deployment & IDE Integration (Zed)
**Difficulty: 4 / 10**
You use **Ollama** or **vLLM** to host your model locally on your server. **Zed** is incredibly fast and has built-in support for "Local LLMs." You just point Zed to your local server's IP address, and your personal AI starts coding with you in-editor with zero lag.
